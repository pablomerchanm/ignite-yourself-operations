§ 01Biographical note

Biographical note

Patrick Brant is a polymer chemist and consultant in polyolefins, based in Houston, Texas. He studied chemistry at George Mason University and took a Ph.D. in inorganic chemistry at the University of Arizona in 1977, followed by post-doctoral research at Purdue University and a National Research Council fellowship at the Naval Research Laboratory in Washington, D.C. He joined Exxon Chemical Company in Baytown, Texas, in 1981, and served ExxonMobil Chemical as Chief Scientist from 2000 to 2011 and as Chief Polymer Science Advisor from 2011 to 2018. His work there included supported metallocene catalysts for gas-phase reactors, the structure–property relationships of polyolefins, and separator film for lithium-ion batteries, developed by a team he led and recognised with the ICIS award for Best Product Innovation in 2008. He is named as inventor on more than 150 US patents and is the author of more than 75 publications. Since 2019 he has worked as an independent consultant and occasional expert witness, collaborates in research with the University of Houston and Baylor University, and founded Molecular Joinery, LLC.

§ 04Scientific work

Scientific work

  1. 01

    Metallocene catalysis

    Design, synthesis, characterisation and operation of supported metallocene catalysts in gas-phase reactors: the research behind the commercialisation of metallocene linear low-density polyethylenes.

  2. 02

    Structure and properties of polyolefins

    The solid and melt state, blends and impact copolymers; chain branching, coil dimensions and melt rheology, published in Macromolecules, 2001 and 2002.

  3. 03

    Battery separator film

    Separator design and fabrication for lithium-ion batteries, from consumer electronics to transportation. ICIS award for Best Product Innovation, 2008.

  4. 04

    Composites and coupling chemistry

    Composite and nanocomposite design, coupling agents and coatings at Molecular Joinery; Grubbs-type ring-opening metathesis polymerisation for plugging abandoned wells, with Baylor University.
