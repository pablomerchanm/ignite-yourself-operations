03A point of view built over four decades

In aviation, the asset is never just the aircraft.

Its condition, maintenance history, technical records, contractual obligations, commercial position and transition requirements have to be understood together. A discrepancy in a maintenance record is rarely only a technical question—it moves value, triggers obligations and decides disputes.

Mark’s work sits at that intersection: establishing what the aircraft and its records actually show, then explaining what that means for the contract and for the asset.

That perspective comes from having done the work. He has held the licence, signed the release to service, run the hangar and built the organisation.

The aircraft, the records and the agreement are one story. They have to be read together.

06For counsel

Aviation disputes turn on what the records can actually support.

Aircraft condition, maintenance history and contractual obligation rarely align neatly by the time a matter reaches counsel. Establishing what happened requires someone who has read these records as the person accountable for them—and who can explain the findings plainly to lawyers, arbitrators and tribunals. Mark provides independent analysis, written reports and expert advice in commercial aviation matters. He is instructed by claimants and defendants alike, and is available as a single joint expert.
