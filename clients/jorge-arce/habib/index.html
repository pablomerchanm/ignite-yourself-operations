§ 01Biographical note

Biographical note

Nagy Habib (born Cairo, 1952) is a British-Egyptian hepatobiliary surgeon and Professor of Hepatobiliary Surgery at Imperial College London, where he has held a chair in the Department of Surgery and Cancer since 2003 and is Academic Head of HPB Surgery. He studied medicine at Ain Shams University, Cairo, trained in surgery with Henri Bismuth and Thomas Starzl, and is a Fellow of the Royal College of Surgeons of Edinburgh. His work spans liver, pancreatic and biliary surgery at Hammersmith Hospital; radiofrequency techniques for liver resection, from which the Habib 4X and Habib EndoHPB instruments were developed; and translational research in liver disease, including early clinical trials of adenovirus gene therapy, autologous stem cells and small activating RNA. From 2007 he served as Imperial’s Pro-Rector for Commercial Affairs. He was elected to the Académie Nationale de Chirurgie in 2015.

§ 04Clinical work

Clinical work

  1. 01

    Hepatopancreatobiliary surgery

    Liver, pancreatic and biliary surgery at Hammersmith Hospital, London.

  2. 02

    Radiofrequency liver resection

    Division of the liver after coagulation of the transection plane by radiofrequency energy, known as Habib’s resection. Published in Annals of Surgery, 2002.

  3. 03

    Living-related liver transplantation

    The first in Africa and the Middle East, 1993; reported in International Surgery the same year.

  4. 04

    Bipolar radiofrequency transection

    Applied to the liver, pancreas and kidney. Digestive Surgery, 2012.
