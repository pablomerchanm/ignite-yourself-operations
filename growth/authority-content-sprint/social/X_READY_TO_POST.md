# X — The Authority Gap | 12 independent posts
**Status:** unpublished drafts; mix over three weeks, 3–4 posts a week. No mass automated reply sequences, no fake engagement.

**X1** A senior career isn't automatically a clear online record. Roles, papers, patents, talks, and projects can be scattered across ten places. The challenge isn't making someone sound impressive. It's making their actual work easier to understand.

**X2** LinkedIn is useful for discovery and relationships. An owned professional website is useful for context, evidence, and continuity. One doesn't need to replace the other.

**X3** A website will not rescue a consulting practice with no distribution. If no qualified buyers are seeing you, the problem may not be the website at all.

**X4** Try this: search your own name in a private window. Could a stranger tell what you work on TODAY, verify the most important claims, and find the correct way to reach you?

**X5** “30 years of experience” is a fact. “Here are the three technical problems I can advise on, and the public work behind that expertise” is much more useful to a buyer.

**X6** An expert witness website should help an attorney evaluate relevant expertise—not bury them in a wall of adjectives. Specialty, credentials, selected public work, source links, safe contact.

**X7** The best professional website may not be the loudest. It may be the easiest to verify.

**X8** If your most important projects are confidential, don't invent portfolio examples. Explain the problems you work on, your methods, your public background, and what you can responsibly share.

**X9** Your old company title may explain your past. It may not explain what someone should hire you to do now.

**X10** Going independent? Define the buyer and the problem before designing the hero section. A beautiful site attached to an unclear offer is still an unclear offer.

**X11** More content ≠ more qualified conversations. Ask: who is this for, what decision does it help them make, and where can they verify the claim?

**X12** Not every established professional needs a new website. If your current profiles do the job, improve them. If the evidence is scattered and buyers can't assess your work, a dedicated professional record may help.
