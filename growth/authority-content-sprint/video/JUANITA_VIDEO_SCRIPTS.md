# Juanita — 3 human-shot short videos for LinkedIn (optionally X)
**Editorial status:** drafts. Juanita should review tone, confirm role before shooting. Do not synthesize her face/voice or claim outcomes from clients. Filming 9:16, medium shot, quiet light, natural speaking pace; avoid artificial HDR look; captions, simple cuts, no manufactured B-roll. 35–60 sec each.

## V1 — “A CV is not a professional record” — 45 sec
**Hook on screen:** “Your experience is real. Is it easy to verify?”

**Script:**
“A lot of accomplished professionals have done important work for decades, but their online presence doesn't show the full picture.
One role is on LinkedIn. Their research is somewhere else. A talk lives on a conference page.
The information exists—it’s just fragmented.
Something we think about at Ignite is how to bring those pieces together so that someone who doesn't already know you can understand what you actually do.
Not to make a career sound bigger. Just to make the work clearer.
If you searched your own name today, would you find the full story?”

**Suggested caption:** “Digital authority isn't always about creating more content. Sometimes it's about organizing what already exists. What would a stranger miss about your work?”
**Visual notes:** one take, tasteful simple room, neutral outfit; optional screenshot of an invented generic schematic (no real prospect pages).

## V2 — “What an attorney needs from an expert profile” — 50 sec
**Hook on screen:** “An expert website should answer questions, not just impress.”

**Script:**
“Imagine someone has to evaluate a specialist they've never met.
They need more than ‘three decades of experience.’
They need to know what the specialist actually works on, which qualifications are verifiable, and where to find public research or publications.
If that requires opening ten different tabs, the issue might be how the information is organized.
A useful professional website creates a clear path through the evidence, with all the right boundaries around confidential work.
What would you want to know first if you were evaluating an expert?”

**Suggested caption:** “The useful question isn't ‘Does the page look premium?’ It’s ‘Can a professional evaluator find the evidence they need?’”
**Review note:** If using legal language, avoid presenting this as legal guidance; “someone evaluating an expert” is safer than claiming every attorney follows same process.

## V3 — “Going independent after 20 years” — 50 sec
**Hook on screen:** “Your former job title is not your new offer.”

**Script:**
“When someone leaves a long corporate career to consult independently, they often already have real credibility.
But a job title like ‘VP’ or ‘Director’ doesn't necessarily tell a new buyer what they can hire that person to do.
So before building a personal website, I'd ask: Who needs your expertise? What decision do you help with? And what real experience can you show to support it?
Once that's clear, the website becomes much easier to build.
And if those questions are still unclear, I'd work on the offer first.”

**Suggested caption:** “From professional experience to independent positioning. The work comes before the page.”

## Production process (30–50 min weekly)
1. Read and rehearse once, make personal edits.
2. Record 2 takes, no teleprompter stare.
3. Add captions (exact wording, proofread).
4. Use a straightforward cover with hook, no invented client result.
5. Post natively to LinkedIn with one question.
6. Reply to relevant comments; tag/link only if naturally relevant.
7. Track production time and qualified conversations.
